# Results and limitations draft

**Counterfactual identity-memory use.** We evaluate whether changing only a supplied miner identity memory changes the intended helmet segmentation while the observation and semantic query remain fixed. On the primary 50-group DOCUMENTED_PROTOCOL_DISJOINT_CONFIRMATION set, CMA achieves 92% CMSA on clean observations and 58% under the fixed target15_b stressor, compared with 4% and 2% for the pinned SegLLM system. Under degradation, CMA retains 84% Memory Fidelity and 3% Identity Error Rate, versus 46% and 38%. These results support stronger use of supplied identity memory for relational segmentation on this evaluated set. [Frozen evidence: comparison summaries](../cycle025/scoring/comparison.json).

**Absolute performance under degradation.** CMA attains 67.53% mIoU under target15_b, exceeding SegLLM's 36.44% by 31.08 percentage points on identical groups. This advantage concerns absolute degraded performance: CMA declines from 88.28% to 67.53% mIoU, a 20.75-point drop, whereas SegLLM declines from 42.71% to 36.44%, a 6.26-point drop. We therefore do not claim lower degradation sensitivity. CMA still fails the strict two-identity criterion on 21 of 50 degraded groups. Historical and development replications are reported separately with their overlap warnings, without pooling them with the primary confirmation. [Frozen evidence: summaries and paired deltas](../cycle025/scoring/comparison.json); [verified table hierarchy](PAPER_LAYER1_TABLE.md).

**System-level comparison.** SegLLM provides a released historical-memory comparator evaluated with the same observations and supplied miner identities, while retaining its pinned native interface. The comparison is between complete systems with different training exposure and domain adaptation, not a controlled substitution of architecture alone. SegLLM's lower clean performance also limits attribution of the gap solely to memory architecture. The counterfactual protocol measures each system's identity-dependent behavior under these common inputs; it does not isolate the effect of an individual CMA component. [Frozen interface and source evidence](../cycle025/protocol_receipt.json); [pinned checkpoint audit](../cycle025/segllm_checkpoint_audit.json).

**Limitations.** The primary set was selected before inference and is disjoint by source-image SHA256 from the reconstructed documented final-stage training and historical evaluation registries. Exact run-bound w15 and ancestor/pretraining exposure remain UNKNOWN, so this is not an exact training-unseen test. Memories are externally supplied and targets are reconstructed pseudo labels; 23 groups retain historical review-level episode QC and 27 are combinations of accepted-high pairs. Structural validity does not substitute for independent annotation. The results do not establish autonomous memory writing, persistence or repair, broader corruption generalization, or safety guarantees. Earlier Layer-2 verifier/controller attempts did not meet their stated requirements and are negative evidence rather than a successful agent contribution.
